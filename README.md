# ORCA – Oil Recovery and Collection Association 🌊

ORCA is an engineering project developed through the **UBC Geering Up STEM Leadership Program** to explore a more efficient and environmentally friendly method of recovering oil from marine spills.

Our team designed and built a small-scale, autonomous oil-recovery vessel capable of collecting surface oil while minimizing the collection of water.

## 🌎 The Problem

Marine oil spills can cause severe and long-lasting damage to aquatic ecosystems, wildlife, coastlines, and surrounding communities.

Our goal was to develop a system that could:

- Rapidly collect oil from the water's surface
- Minimize water collection
- Be reusable rather than disposable
- Remain stable on the water
- Be compact enough for potential deployment aboard vessels
- Operate with minimal human intervention

## 🚤 Our Solution

ORCA uses a **catamaran-style hull**, consisting of two parallel hulls connected by a central platform.

The catamaran configuration provides:

- High stability on the water
- Space for electronics and oil storage
- A central channel that naturally directs surface oil toward the collection system

The prototype was designed as a compact platform that could potentially be stored aboard ships and rapidly deployed following an oil spill.

## 🛢️ Oil Collection System

At the center of ORCA is its oil extraction mechanism.

A **hydrophobic foam roller** comes into contact with the surface of the water. Because the material attracts oil while repelling water, oil adheres to the roller as it rotates.

The process works continuously:

1. The vessel travels across the contaminated surface.
2. Surface oil is directed between the catamaran hulls.
3. A hydrophobic roller selectively collects the oil.
4. A scraper removes the oil from the roller.
5. Recovered oil is transferred into an onboard storage system.
6. The roller rotates back into the water and repeats the process.

During prototype testing, the system was able to remove approximately **90% of surface oil while minimizing water absorption**.

## ⚙️ Propulsion System

ORCA also required a compact method of transferring power from the motor to the propeller.

We designed a **four-gear gearbox** that redirects rotational motion through the vessel and transfers it to the propeller.

The propeller converts this rotational motion into thrust, allowing the prototype to move across the surface of the water.

## 🧑‍💻 Engineering Process

Our development process followed an iterative engineering design approach:

1. Identified marine oil spills as the environmental problem we wanted to address
2. Researched existing oil-recovery technologies
3. Brainstormed four possible solutions
4. Compared the advantages and disadvantages of each concept
5. Narrowed our designs to a skimmer and filtration system
6. Selected the skimmer-based design
7. Divided the team into boat and collection-system groups
8. Produced digital sketches and early designs
9. Created components using CAD
10. 3D printed prototype components
11. Assembled and tested the vessel
12. Iterated on the design based on testing

ORCA gave our team experience taking an engineering project from an initial environmental problem to a functioning physical prototype.

We learned how to:

- Evaluate competing engineering solutions
- Design mechanical components using CAD
- Prototype using 3D printing
- Apply hydrophobic materials to oil-water separation
- Design for buoyancy and stability
- Test and improve a physical prototype
- Divide a complex engineering problem between specialized teams

Most importantly, the project demonstrated how mechanical engineering, materials science, and environmental science can be combined to address a real-world problem.
